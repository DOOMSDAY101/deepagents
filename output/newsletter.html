This Week in Music

Here's a look at the latest developments across Rock, Latin, Metal, and Alternative & Punk scenes.

Rock

Rock’s pulse is beating strong with a mix of legacy reissues and fresh arrivals. The Toadies finally unveiled The Charmer on May 1, their first album in nearly a decade, recorded by the late Steve Albini and released via Austin’s Spaceflight Records. Lou Gramm, the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame–indicated Foreigner frontman, dropped the blistering single “Young Love” ahead of his solo album Released (landing March 27, 2026), promising a tour that pairs solo dates with Foreigner appearances. Record Store Day 2026 is stocked with rock gems: Bruce Springsteen’s Live From Asbury Park 2024, Cream’s Wheels of Fire live set, and a limited‑picture‑disc edition of Alice Cooper’s The Revenge of Alice Cooper. Meanwhile, Finnish hard‑rockers Temple Balls released their self‑titled studio album on February 20, and Aerosmith treated fans to a remastered “Legacy Edition” of their 1973 debut on March 20. Even pop provocateur Charli XCX is flirting with rock on her forthcoming Music, Fashion, Film, proving the genre’s reach extends far beyond its traditional borders.

Latin

Latin music continues its explosive growth in 2024, with Regional Mexican leading the charge—artists like Peso Pluma, Fuerza Regida, and Junior H each surpassed 100 million U.S. on-demand audio streams in the first half of the year, according to Luminate’s midyear report. Meanwhile, mainstream stars are pushing boundaries: J Balvin’s August album Rayo blends Latin pop with urban edge, while Prince Royce’s February single “Llamada Perdida” leans into club-driven bachata. Kali Uchis’ January release Orquídeas further cements her genre-fluid artistry, and Quevedo’s introspective sophomore effort Buenas Noches (2024) earned critical acclaim alongside his Latin GRAMMY win. Beyond the charts, cumbia is experiencing a resurgence, fueled by collaborations like Los Ángeles Azules with Santa Fe Klan and Maria Becerra. Live, the Sueños Music Festival returned to Chicago’s Grant Park over Memorial Day, drawing massive crowds for two days of reggaeton and Latin culture. With streaming delivering 98% of Latin revenues and the genre now the fastest-growing in the U.S., the momentum shows no signs of slowing.

Metal

September’s metal landscape brims with veteran resurgences and critical darlings. Accept’s career-spanning Teutonic Titans 1976-2026 cracked the UK Rock & Metal Albums Top 12, proving enduring appeal, while Amon Arath’s The Allfather Awakens continues their melodic death metal dominance via Metal Blade. Meanwhile, Down’s long-awaited Volume V arrives November 20th on Nuclear Blast, promising sludge-heavy revival.

Critics are raving: Spirit Adrift’s Infinite Illumination earned Pitchfork acclaim for its doom-laden grandeur, Neurosis’ An Undying Love for a Burning World landed Best New Album honors, and Worm’s Necropalace and Converge’s Love Is Not Enough drew deep praise for pushing extreme metal’s boundaries. Blood Incantation’s “Inner Paths (to Outer Space)” snagged Best New Track, highlighting prog-death’s continued vitality.

Underground scenes are thriving too—Bandcamp Daily spotlighted Brazil’s metal vanguard, where acts blend grindcore, D-beat, and Afro-Cuban rhythms into feral new hybrids. With over 90 new releases flooding September alone (from Carnation’s Symphony Of Flesh to Caspian’s self-titled), the genre shows no signs of slowing, balancing legacy acts with innovative frontier-pushers.

Alternative & Punk

In early 2024, the punk underground kept its momentum with a flurry of urgent releases. UK artist Rat Boy returned with the raw-edged single “Broken,” a preview of his forthcoming album Crash! that leans into mid‑90s punk influences and lo‑fi production. Meanwhile, hardcore‑tinged act AMORIST dropped the aggressive new track “SCALPEL,” and metallers AMIENSUS unveiled the lead single “London (Love To Hate)” ahead of their April 2024 EP Reclamation: Part 1. Across the Atlantic, the jazz‑punk trio The Messthetics reunited with saxophonist James Brandon Lewis for a second collaborative LP, while pop‑punk provocateur Willow Smith surprised fans with the self‑produced Petal Rock Black, blending ska‑punk energy with indie experimentation. Veteran acts are not resting either: Buzzcocks announced Attitude Adjustment, a Motown‑tinged punk record, and The Overjoyed prepared a self‑titled album for a February 2026 release, promising a London headline show. Together, these releases highlight a scene that balances nostalgic grit with forward‑looking genre‑bending.
